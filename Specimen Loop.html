How the loop is built

1Tone

The photo is centre-cropped to a square, pulled toward monochrome, pushed with contrast, and given fine grain. The reference clip is near-black-and-white on a pale grey ground, so the default saturation is zero.

2Flow warp

A stream function ψ is built from layered simplex noise. Its rotated gradient ∇⊥ψ is a divergence-free velocity field, which is why the flesh smears and swirls like liquid instead of bulging. Each pixel is pushed through the field in S small steps, and more steps give longer, more stretched streaks.

3Why it loops

Time never enters the noise directly. It enters as R·cos θ and R·sin θ, where θ = 2πt/T. When t reaches T the field is exactly where it started, so the first and last frames join without a cut. R sets how far the field travels per loop.

4Mask and shear

The focus mask M limits the warp to a soft disc around the point you click, optionally weighted to bright tones so skin moves more than the background. Shear bands slide thin horizontal slices left or right, the striated look in the reference.

5Tracker network

Anchor points come from the strongest edges in the photo. Each node hops between anchors H times per loop, drifts on a small Lissajous path, and shows a word and a 7-digit value. Lines join each node to L others.

6Glitch bursts

Bursts cut to black with a thresholded pixel mosaic, then stack photo windows with ghost outlines, orange tag strips and counter bars. Bursts sit at fixed points inside the loop, so they repeat identically on every pass.
